# Roots
<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform
web

## Stack
Buildless HTML, CSS, and JavaScript for the published static demonstration. An optional Python standard-library bridge serves the same dashboard locally, stores synchronized snapshots and assistant drafts in SQLite, and connects to Gemini. Private Telegram polling is available when explicitly configured and started.

## Users
Nonprofit coordinators managing volunteers, environmental projects and opportunities.

## Product Purpose

Connect volunteer engagement to useful benefits, learning, partnerships, and mission-aligned project support. The front page presents a readable intelligence briefing: a few connected findings, inspectable source records, expandable community patterns, and a conversation entry point beneath them. Coordinators can inspect the records behind a pattern, review a suggested action, or ask Roots to develop a next step.

## Capabilities and Constraints
The four primary destinations are Overview, People, Opportunities, and Notices. Overview retains the `#briefing` route, with `#overview` as an alias. A single 680px reading column begins with a count-aware heading—“Two things worth a look.” for two visible findings—and independent review status. Each finding follows one order: title, short explanation, contributing records, and a useful next step beside an evidence disclosure. A third connection and Community patterns are collapsed rows beneath. Participation, Interests, and Events tabs and inline record counts remain inspectable when opened. A full-width 680px composer beneath the findings opens the existing Ask Roots conversation. Ask Roots and Calendar remain secondary destinations. The sidebar is 174px and collapses to 60px. Opportunities and People Groups use 1000px containers with narrative on the left and clickable avatar clusters and recorded evidence on the right; these open rows stack on mobile. People Everyone retains a 1080px table container, and Notices retains its 900px dated feed. The shared page container is 1184px. The seed dataset contains 35 volunteers, 12 opportunity leads (11 conceptual candidates and one sourced public policy), and three project drafts.

Participation separates people with one visit, repeat visits, no visits, and unknown counts; its percentage is the share with repeat visits among profiles with at least one recorded visit. It is not a cohort return or retention rate. Interests counts people with each stated interest, so categories can overlap. Clicking chart groups or inline counts opens relevant records. The seed snapshot contains 8 one-visit profiles, 27 repeat-visit profiles, and 893 recorded hours. Totals update from saved records without inventing history or change over time. Unknown values stay separate from zero. The Events tab shows up to three event rows and their recorded capacity; aggregate attendance does not identify individual RSVPs. Calendar remains available through secondary navigation.

RootsJournal stores browser-local first-observed, meaningful-update, and checked timestamps per scope in `roots-review-journal-v1`. Manual checks update checked time; unchanged entry content keeps its existing dates. These are observations of saved records, not newly discovered external information or verified publication dates. The interface does not automate external research. The Microsoft policy check is a separately dated, manually sourced reference.

The briefing cites the records behind each suggestion and makes missing information explicit. In the static demo, suggestions are deterministic record matches. With the local bridge, Gemini independently selects and connects a bounded catalog of derived facts and proposes concise interpretations and next steps. It does not use chat history or conduct external research. Positive project possibilities connect current people, supported proposed roles, a project, and an optional related support lead; they suppress a duplicate coordination warning for the same project. Service-hour findings connect the interest to a school-partnership pathway when that lead exists. The server validates strict fact IDs and rebuilds factual summaries, numeric evidence, and citations from the catalog. Model interpretation still requires judgment; reference validation does not prove a suggestion is correct. Raw contact details and coordinator notes are excluded from this review.

Recheck after meaningful record changes, every 30 minutes while the app is open, when it becomes visible, or on manual refresh. Generation has pending, reviewing, ready, and failed states. Keep the previous successful briefing visible during review or failure, identify retained stale evidence, and discard results generated against superseded data. A failed attempt retries through explicit refresh or meaningful data change rather than an endless loop. A small three-dot indicator in the freshness line accompanies actual checking work, with no artificial delay and no animation under reduced motion. A check is not a discovery: unchanged inputs must not generate invented findings, fresh-looking activity, or novelty badges. Keep review time compact and scope in the explanation disclosure; cached IDs are derived from selected facts. There is no dismiss feature or promised monitoring after the app closes.

People defaults to Groups and Ways to take part. The seed starts with five people who explicitly list service hours and eight with one recorded visit. Each open row pairs a conversation starter with an avatar cluster, the group’s share of all people, direct recorded hours, one-visit count, and any confirmed target count; missing values retain coverage information. Student, employer, and confirmed service-hour target groups appear only from explicit recorded context. No student or employer identity is seeded or inferred from interests, names, email, or hours. Interests, Participation, and Age groups remain available. Interest groups sort by count then label and show four initially; the gardening group explicitly means eight of 35 people named that interest. Member and related-lead disclosures remain available. Participation separates one, repeat, zero, and unknown visits without inferring recency or retention. Age groups use only direct ageGroup or ageBand text; no age ranges are seeded. Everyone retains the searchable table, existing filters, and Add volunteer. Profiles retain Profile, Activity, and Notes. People context & preferences now includes Student status (Not recorded, Student, Not a student), optional School / college and Employer, alongside availability, declared skills, contact preference, and confirmed hour targets. Values come from direct profile fields or actual local overrides, retain provenance, and become unknown when cleared. Notes and other saved preferences remain preserved. Illustrative history, approved/pending hours, and example targets remain labeled and do not establish cohort membership or verified progress. Contact preference is not messaging consent.

Opportunities defaults to For your community and Participation ideas. The first three proposals connect a four-week series of two-hour supervised service shifts, a growing series with a potential tool-library benefit, and practical training followed by another volunteer session to relevant people. The narrative gives a proposed activity, possible benefit, concrete steps, and terms to confirm. Beside it, avatar clusters open actual profiles and evidence shows matching counts, one-visit counts, recorded contribution, and shared themes. The service-hour proposal has five interest matches, three with one visit, and 32 recorded hours; student status and school requirements need individual confirmation. Three ideas appear initially, with three more on request. Most people sorts by distinct matching profiles. Closest match first sorts by profiles matching at least two themes, then total people. These are deterministic ordering rules, not AI confidence or predicted retention. Funding & support filters grants, fellowships, funded partnerships, in-kind and employer routes without ranking projected income. The separate Microsoft policy route remains visible regardless of this filter and does not fabricate employee avatars. All leads, Projects, and Saved preserve 12 leads and three draft plans, audience filters, search, saves, native disclosures, detailed sheets, and four-at-a-time library results. Opportunities uses one actual local record-check timestamp above its tabs. Interest matches establish neither agreement nor eligibility.

The Microsoft lead (`o12`) records an official public employee-giving policy checked on 24 October 2026, separate from the 11 conceptual leads. It offers $25 per eligible volunteer hour within a combined $15,000 annual match limit per employee across time and giving. Sources: [Microsoft’s community-involvement policy](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/reno/community-involvement) and its [FY25 nonprofit employee-engagement guide](https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/msc/documents/presentations/CSR/FY25-Max-Employee-Engagement-March-2025.pdf). The proposed route is to confirm nonprofit eligibility and Benevity registration, prepare an activity with supervision and hour records, find a willing employee or Change Agent, and have employees submit their eligible hours. The guide does not accept unsolicited grant or event-sponsorship requests. Roots’ nonprofit eligibility and any worker’s eligibility are unconfirmed. An employer recorded as Microsoft only supports a contact to ask; it does not establish access to the benefit. The stated cap is a policy limit, not expected revenue.

Notices is a primary destination with Notes, Drafts, Workflows, and Delivery views. Notes is the default dated feed, with expandable Notes & people, source profiles, review actions, and a Reviewed filter. Ask Roots can also surface an inline action draft with its recipient, unsent state, and delivery-readiness blockers. Edit message saves changes to the unsent backend draft; Copy places its text on the clipboard; Refine in chat prepares a follow-up request. None delivers the message. Workflow rules record an audience trigger, delay, channel, and message for review; saving does not activate or schedule them. SMS draft audiences exclude people without recorded permission. Delivery reports missing providers and stored contact/permission coverage, not verified deliverability. No outreach has been sent. Email consent, delivery details, and trustworthy timing records still need verification before future activation.

The published static demo has no assistant backend and no email/SMS delivery. Its record-based insights and draft preparation must be distinguished from live model responses. Browser-local records are separate by origin. The local bridge receives browser snapshots and gives Gemini constrained access to relevant demo records and draft creation; assistant drafts and activity return through the interface. It does not turn the published site into a connected service, reconcile multiple browsers, deliver outreach, or apply for funding. Telegram shares the local assistant when a bot, allowed user, and polling are configured; configuration alone is not a verified connection. Both web and Telegram must keep outbound email/SMS drafts visibly unsent.

## Product Principles
Make the current community and its next useful actions easy to understand. Compute analytics from saved records and make the contributing people inspectable. Distinguish snapshot composition from historical retention or growth. Surface a small number of supported recommendations without requiring a prompt, keep their review independent of conversation history, and distinguish facts from model interpretation. Explain why a person or project may fit and what remains unconfirmed. Capture participation preferences from the person rather than inferring them. Keep drafts visibly unsent, checks separate from new findings, workflow activation separate from configuration, and server credentials out of the frontend.
