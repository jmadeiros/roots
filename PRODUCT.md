# Roots
<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform
web

## Stack
Buildless HTML, CSS, and JavaScript for the published static demonstration. An optional Python standard-library bridge serves the same dashboard locally, stores synchronized snapshots and assistant drafts in SQLite, and connects to Gemini. Private Telegram polling is available when explicitly configured and started.

## Users
Nonprofit coordinators managing volunteers, environmental projects and opportunities.

## Product Purpose

Connect volunteer engagement to useful benefits, learning, partnerships, and mission-aligned project support. The front page presents a readable intelligence briefing: a few connected findings, inspectable source records, expandable community patterns, and a conversation entry point beneath them. Coordinators can inspect the records behind a pattern, review a suggested action, or ask Roots to develop a next step.

## Capabilities and Constraints
The four primary destinations are Overview, People, Opportunities, and Notices. Overview retains the `#briefing` route, with `#overview` as an alias. A single 680px reading column begins with a count-aware heading—“Two things worth a look.” for two visible findings—and independent review status. Each finding follows one order: title, short explanation, contributing records, and a useful next step beside an evidence disclosure. A third connection and Community patterns are collapsed rows beneath. Participation, Interests, and Events tabs and inline record counts remain inspectable when opened. A full-width 680px composer beneath the findings opens the existing Ask Roots conversation. Ask Roots and Calendar remain secondary destinations. The sidebar is 174px and collapses to 60px. Opportunities and People Groups share the 680px reading width; People Everyone retains a 1080px table container, and Notices retains its 900px dated feed. The shared page container is 1184px. The seed dataset contains 35 volunteers, 11 candidate opportunity leads, and three project drafts.

Participation separates people with one visit, repeat visits, no visits, and unknown counts; its percentage is the share with repeat visits among profiles with at least one recorded visit. It is not a cohort return or retention rate. Interests counts people with each stated interest, so categories can overlap. Clicking chart groups or inline counts opens relevant records. The seed snapshot contains 8 one-visit profiles, 27 repeat-visit profiles, and 893 recorded hours. Totals update from saved records without inventing history or change over time. Unknown values stay separate from zero. The Events tab shows up to three event rows and their recorded capacity; aggregate attendance does not identify individual RSVPs. Calendar remains available through secondary navigation.

RootsJournal stores browser-local first-observed, meaningful-update, and checked timestamps per scope in `roots-review-journal-v1`. Manual checks update checked time; unchanged entry content keeps its existing dates. These are observations of saved records, not newly discovered external information or verified publication dates. The interface does not implement external research.

The briefing cites the records behind each suggestion and makes missing information explicit. In the static demo, suggestions are deterministic record matches. With the local bridge, Gemini independently selects and connects a bounded catalog of derived facts and proposes concise interpretations and next steps. It does not use chat history or conduct external research. Positive project possibilities connect current people, supported proposed roles, a project, and an optional related support lead; they suppress a duplicate coordination warning for the same project. Service-hour findings connect the interest to a school-partnership pathway when that lead exists. The server validates strict fact IDs and rebuilds factual summaries, numeric evidence, and citations from the catalog. Model interpretation still requires judgment; reference validation does not prove a suggestion is correct. Raw contact details and coordinator notes are excluded from this review.

Recheck after meaningful record changes, every 30 minutes while the app is open, when it becomes visible, or on manual refresh. Generation has pending, reviewing, ready, and failed states. Keep the previous successful briefing visible during review or failure, identify retained stale evidence, and discard results generated against superseded data. A failed attempt retries through explicit refresh or meaningful data change rather than an endless loop. A small three-dot indicator in the freshness line accompanies actual checking work, with no artificial delay and no animation under reduced motion. A check is not a discovery: unchanged inputs must not generate invented findings, fresh-looking activity, or novelty badges. Keep review time compact and scope in the explanation disclosure; cached IDs are derived from selected facts. There is no dismiss feature or promised monitoring after the app closes.

People defaults to Groups, with Interests, Participation, and Age groups views. Interest groups use distinct stated interests, sort by people count then label, and show the four largest groups initially with Show all available. A native disclosure opens the group’s people and candidate leads connected by that stated interest. Participation separates one, repeat, zero, and unknown recorded visits without inferring recency or retention. Age groups use only explicit ageGroup or ageBand text on the person record; no ages are inferred, and the seed records have no age ranges. Everyone preserves the searchable table, existing All people / Active / First visit filters, and Add volunteer. Individual profiles retain Profile, Activity, and Notes views. Stated interests and declared skills remain distinct. Coordinators can record availability, a preferred contact channel, a confirmed service-hour target, and notes. Seed history and approved/pending-hour breakdowns are illustrative and labeled accordingly. Contact preferences do not grant messaging consent.

Opportunities defaults to For your community, where RootsConnections ranks all matching candidate leads around the people who could benefit or participate. Each possibility leads with a people count and possible outcome, shows three named profile links and a link to remaining matches, and expands Why these people for theme counts, closer overlaps, a related project when present, and next steps. Three possibilities appear initially, with three more on request. Most people ranks by distinct matching profiles. Closest match ranks first by profiles matching at least two distinct themes, then by total matching people. Funding & support includes grants, fellowships, explicitly funded partnerships, in-kind benefits, and employer benefits; it filters the set but does not rank financial value because award amounts are unverified. All leads, Projects, and Saved preserve the source library. Eleven candidate leads cover benefits, training, funded placements, partnerships, and project support. Three project drafts suggest people and roles, budgets, funding routes, and readiness checks. The 680px single column uses one actual record-check timestamp above its tabs, without per-lead date rails. All leads offers audience filters, and the library retains search, saves, four-at-a-time results, native expandable summaries, and detailed sheets with requirements, people, budgets, and next steps. Interest matching does not establish eligibility. Official program links are references to check, not evidence of a secured benefit or open application.

Notices is a primary destination with Notes, Drafts, Workflows, and Delivery views. Notes is the default dated feed, with expandable Notes & people, source profiles, review actions, and a Reviewed filter. Ask Roots can also surface an inline action draft with its recipient, unsent state, and delivery-readiness blockers. Edit message saves changes to the unsent backend draft; Copy places its text on the clipboard; Refine in chat prepares a follow-up request. None delivers the message. Workflow rules record an audience trigger, delay, channel, and message for review; saving does not activate or schedule them. SMS draft audiences exclude people without recorded permission. Delivery reports missing providers and stored contact/permission coverage, not verified deliverability. No outreach has been sent. Email consent, delivery details, and trustworthy timing records still need verification before future activation.

The published static demo has no assistant backend and no email/SMS delivery. Its record-based insights and draft preparation must be distinguished from live model responses. Browser-local records are separate by origin. The local bridge receives browser snapshots and gives Gemini constrained access to relevant demo records and draft creation; assistant drafts and activity return through the interface. It does not turn the published site into a connected service, reconcile multiple browsers, deliver outreach, or apply for funding. Telegram shares the local assistant when a bot, allowed user, and polling are configured; configuration alone is not a verified connection. Both web and Telegram must keep outbound email/SMS drafts visibly unsent.

## Product Principles
Make the current community and its next useful actions easy to understand. Compute analytics from saved records and make the contributing people inspectable. Distinguish snapshot composition from historical retention or growth. Surface a small number of supported recommendations without requiring a prompt, keep their review independent of conversation history, and distinguish facts from model interpretation. Explain why a person or project may fit and what remains unconfirmed. Capture participation preferences from the person rather than inferring them. Keep drafts visibly unsent, checks separate from new findings, workflow activation separate from configuration, and server credentials out of the frontend.
