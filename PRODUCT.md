# Roots
<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform
web

## Stack
Buildless HTML, CSS, and JavaScript for the published static demonstration. An optional Python standard-library bridge serves the same dashboard locally, stores synchronized snapshots and assistant drafts in SQLite, and connects to Gemini. Private Telegram polling is available when explicitly configured and started.

## Users
Nonprofit coordinators managing volunteers, environmental projects and opportunities.

## Product Purpose
Connect volunteer engagement to useful benefits, learning, partnerships, and mission-aligned project support. The front page builds on the original darker forest-green Overview dashboard, combining a current community snapshot, useful analytics, independent AI recommendations, and upcoming events. Coordinators can inspect the records behind a pattern, review a suggested action, or open a separate conversation for help.

## Capabilities and Constraints
The four primary destinations are Overview, People, Opportunities, and Outreach. Overview retains the `#briefing` route, with `#overview` as an alias. Three compact metrics show people, recorded hours, and opportunity leads. Below them, the wider left column contains two recommendation cards with an optional third; the first uses a sage surface and forest action. “Why this surfaced” reveals supporting records, unknowns, and suggested next steps. Independent review status sits beside the recommendation heading. A right column contains Community patterns and Coming up. Ask Roots and Calendar remain secondary destinations, with a compact composer under the recommendations. The desktop page container is capped at 1184px and the sidebar is 200px, reducing to 180px on smaller desktops and 60px when collapsed. The seed dataset contains 35 volunteers, 11 candidate opportunity leads, and three project drafts.

Community patterns switches between Participation and Interests. Participation separates people with one visit, repeat visits, no visits, and unknown counts; its percentage is the share with repeat visits among profiles with at least one recorded visit. It is not a cohort return or retention rate. Interests counts people with each stated interest, so categories can overlap. Clicking chart groups or snapshot metrics opens the relevant records. The seed snapshot contains 8 one-visit profiles, 27 repeat-visit profiles, and 893 recorded hours. Totals update from saved records without inventing history or change over time. Unknown values stay separate from zero. Coming up shows three event rows with valid recorded capacity and a route to the calendar; aggregate attendance does not identify individual RSVPs.

The briefing cites the records behind each suggestion and makes missing information explicit. In the static demo, suggestions are deterministic record matches. With the local bridge, Gemini independently selects and connects a bounded catalog of derived facts and proposes concise interpretations and next steps. It does not use chat history or conduct external research. The server validates strict fact IDs and rebuilds factual summaries, numeric evidence, and citations from the catalog. Model interpretation still requires judgment; reference validation does not prove a suggestion is correct. Raw contact details and coordinator notes are excluded from this review.

Recheck after meaningful record changes, every 30 minutes while the app is open, when it becomes visible, or on manual refresh. Generation has pending, reviewing, ready, and failed states. Keep the previous successful briefing visible during review or failure, identify retained stale evidence, and discard results generated against superseded data. A failed attempt retries through explicit refresh or meaningful data change rather than an endless loop. A small three-dot indicator in the freshness line accompanies actual checking work, with no artificial delay and no animation under reduced motion. A check is not a discovery: unchanged inputs must not generate invented findings, fresh-looking activity, or novelty badges. Keep review time compact and scope in the explanation disclosure; cached IDs are derived from selected facts. There is no dismiss feature or promised monitoring after the app closes.

People preserves the searchable table and expands individual profiles with Profile, Activity, and Notes views. Stated interests and declared skills remain distinct. Coordinators can record availability, a preferred contact channel, a confirmed service-hour target, and notes. Seed history and approved/pending-hour breakdowns are illustrative and labeled accordingly. Contact preferences do not grant messaging consent.

Opportunities has For volunteers, For organization, Projects, and Saved views. Eleven candidate leads cover benefits, training, funded placements, partnerships, and project support. Three project drafts suggest people and roles, budgets, funding routes, and readiness checks. Searchable compact rows lead to details with collapsed requirements and next steps. Interest matching does not establish eligibility. Official program links are references to check, not evidence of a secured benefit or open application.

Outreach is a primary destination with Drafts, Workflows, and Delivery views. Ask Roots can also surface an inline action draft with its recipient, unsent state, and delivery-readiness blockers. Edit message saves changes to the unsent backend draft; Copy places its text on the clipboard; Refine in chat prepares a follow-up request. None delivers the message. Workflow rules record an audience trigger, delay, channel, and message for review; saving does not activate or schedule them. SMS draft audiences exclude people without recorded permission. Delivery reports missing providers and stored contact/permission coverage, not verified deliverability. No outreach has been sent. Email consent, delivery details, and trustworthy timing records still need verification before future activation.

The published static demo has no assistant backend and no email/SMS delivery. Its record-based insights and draft preparation must be distinguished from live model responses. Browser-local records are separate by origin. The local bridge receives browser snapshots and gives Gemini constrained access to relevant demo records and draft creation; assistant drafts and activity return through the interface. It does not turn the published site into a connected service, reconcile multiple browsers, deliver outreach, or apply for funding. Telegram shares the local assistant when a bot, allowed user, and polling are configured; configuration alone is not a verified connection. Both web and Telegram must keep outbound email/SMS drafts visibly unsent.

## Brand Commitments
Roots name, sprout identity, self-hosted DM Sans, warm paper, dark forest selected navigation and primary actions, sage featured recommendations, and white analytics cards. The original darker dashboard is the visual authority. Keep its clear hierarchy while making metrics compact and patterns inspectable. Overview uses a 30px heading, 34px metric values, 20–22px recommendation titles, and 13px supporting copy. The independent AI review and separate Ask Roots conversation support the dashboard; they do not turn the front page into a narrow chat column.

## Product Principles
Make the current community and its next useful actions easy to understand. Compute analytics from saved records and make the contributing people inspectable. Distinguish snapshot composition from historical retention or growth. Surface a small number of supported recommendations without requiring a prompt, keep their review independent of conversation history, and distinguish facts from model interpretation. Explain why a person or project may fit and what remains unconfirmed. Capture participation preferences from the person rather than inferring them. Keep drafts visibly unsent, checks separate from new findings, workflow activation separate from configuration, and server credentials out of the frontend.
