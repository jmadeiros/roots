# Roots
<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform
web

## Stack
Buildless HTML, CSS, and JavaScript for the published static demonstration. An optional Python standard-library bridge serves the same dashboard locally, stores synchronized snapshots and assistant drafts in SQLite, and connects to Gemini. Private Telegram polling is available when explicitly configured and started.

## Users
Nonprofit coordinators managing volunteers, environmental projects and opportunities.

## Product Purpose

Connect volunteer engagement to useful benefits, learning, partnerships, and mission-aligned project support. The front page combines a time-aware greeting and observed daily changes with community analytics on the left, two dated connected findings on the right, and a conversation entry point below. Coordinators can inspect contributing records, review a suggested action, or ask Roots to develop the next step.

## Capabilities and Constraints
The four primary destinations are Overview, People, Opportunities, and Notices. Overview retains the `#briefing` route, with `#overview` as an alias. Its 1020px container begins with a local-time greeting to Jamie, current date, and Today / Last 7 days controls for actual locally observed changes. A desktop grid places Community at a glance analytics on the left and two dated findings on the right; an optional third finding remains a disclosure. Each finding shows title, Noted or Updated date, explanation, contributing records, and an action beside Why this surfaced. Participation stays visible, with Interests / Events tabs beneath it. A full-width conversation section below both columns opens Ask Roots. On phones the order is greeting and changes, findings, analytics, then conversation. Ask Roots and Calendar remain secondary destinations. The sidebar is 204px and collapses to 60px. Opportunities and People Groups retain 1000px containers with narrative beside tightly overlapping initials and a +N full-members control; these rows stack on mobile. People Everyone retains a 1080px table, Ask Roots a 720px conversation, Notices a 900px dated feed, and the shared page container is 1184px. The dataset contains 35 fictional volunteers, seven programme or local partnership routes and three project drafts.

Participation separates people with one visit, repeat visits, no visits, and unknown counts; its percentage is the share with repeat visits among profiles with at least one recorded visit. It is not a cohort return or retention rate. Interests counts people with each stated interest, so categories can overlap. Clicking chart groups or inline counts opens relevant records. The seed snapshot contains 8 one-visit profiles, 27 repeat-visit profiles, and 893 recorded hours. Totals update from saved records without inventing history or change over time. Unknown values stay separate from zero. The Events tab shows up to three event rows and their recorded capacity; aggregate attendance does not identify individual RSVPs. Calendar remains available through secondary navigation.

The daily observation ledger (`roots-daily-v1`) starts with existing records as its baseline; it does not present seeded records as new arrivals or invent earlier activity. Later saved additions, updates and removals to people, events and opportunities become local observations. Dated assistant drafts created after tracking began and confirmed owner-only Telegram demo receipts can also appear; a demo receipt is distinct from volunteer outreach. Today uses local midnight; Last 7 days includes today and the previous six local calendar days. Up to three entries appear first, with See all for the rest. The ledger retains up to 300 entries, uses an honest empty state, and is scoped to this browser and origin. A late-arriving collection establishes its own baseline; repeated observations and legacy relative-time labels do not create activity.

Programme terms were researched from official sources on 24 October 2026. Their source-check date is separate from browser-local observation and fit-check timestamps. Recheck fit recalculates against current community records; it does not browse, refresh terms or run automatic external searches. Published deadlines become a passed-deadline label after their date. Upcoming or campus-specific windows remain explicitly qualified. The local school route has no external-source verification date. RootsJournal retains separate first-observed, meaningful-update and checked timestamps in `roots-review-journal-v1`.

The briefing cites the records behind each suggestion and makes missing information explicit. In the static demo, suggestions are deterministic record matches. With the local bridge, Gemini independently selects and connects a bounded catalog of derived facts and proposes concise interpretations and next steps. It does not use chat history or conduct external research. Positive project possibilities connect current people, supported proposed roles, a project, and an optional related support lead; they suppress a duplicate coordination warning for the same project. Service-hour findings connect the interest to a school-partnership pathway when that lead exists. The server validates strict fact IDs and rebuilds factual summaries, numeric evidence, and citations from the catalog. Model interpretation still requires judgment; reference validation does not prove a suggestion is correct. Raw contact details and coordinator notes are excluded from this review.

Recheck after meaningful record changes, every 30 minutes while the app is open, when it becomes visible, or on manual refresh. Generation has pending, reviewing, ready, and failed states. Keep the previous successful briefing visible during review or failure, identify retained stale evidence, and discard results generated against superseded data. A failed attempt retries through explicit refresh or meaningful data change rather than an endless loop. A small three-dot indicator in the freshness line accompanies actual checking work, with no artificial delay and no animation under reduced motion. A check is not a discovery: unchanged inputs must not generate invented findings, fresh-looking activity, or novelty badges. Keep review time compact and scope in the explanation disclosure; cached IDs are derived from selected facts. There is no dismiss feature or promised monitoring after the app closes.

Notices is a primary destination with Notes, Drafts, Workflows, and Delivery views. Notes remains the default dated feed. Ask Roots presents saved SMS/email drafts in People → Message → Review: choose intended recipients, edit each message, save changes and review the content. The primary Review action is **Send demo to my Telegram**. All demo messages go to the configured owner’s private Telegram chat, labeled with their intended recipients; no SMS or email reaches those people, and the underlying drafts remain unsent by those channels. Real contact and permission requirements remain visible under **Before sending to real recipients** and do not block owner-only Telegram delivery. Review stays unavailable while selected messages have unsaved edits. Copy and Refine in chat remain available. The labeled browser-only sample cannot send, even when a transport is configured. Manually written Notices drafts stay local on Save draft; Review demo saves one personalized server draft per selected person, then opens the same Review workbench without sending. Workflow settings remain inactive drafts; no automatic volunteer outreach is enabled.

The published static demo has no live assistant bridge or outgoing-message route, even when its hosting is private. Its deterministic record matches and editable sample plans remain distinct from live model responses. The optional local bridge synchronizes records and saves shared drafts. The dedicated Telegram setup targets `@roots_community_josh_bot`: after Start, use ordinary language without a `/roots` prefix. An explicit current send request can authorize delivery of one to ten saved draft messages to the owner only; asking only for a draft does not send. The server accepts saved draft IDs within the conversation’s authorized scope or explicitly identified by the user, fixes the Telegram destination server-side, and requires the authenticated browser session and anti-CSRF token for web send requests. Telegram requests come from the allowlisted owner’s private chat. Receipt status distinguishes sent, partial, sending and failed; persistent identity and duplicate protection prevent reloads or repeated requests from silently resending. Failed or uncertain delivery never auto-retries; a pre-dispatch authorization rejection may refresh the session but requires another explicit request. No route delivers volunteer SMS/email, applies for funding, or makes the static site a live service.

## Product Principles
Make the current community and its next useful actions easy to understand. Compute analytics from saved records and make the contributing people inspectable. Distinguish snapshot composition from historical retention or growth. Surface a small number of supported recommendations without requiring a prompt, keep their review independent of conversation history, and distinguish facts from model interpretation. Explain why a person or project may fit and what remains unconfirmed. Capture participation preferences from the person rather than inferring them. Keep SMS/email drafts visibly unsent and owner-only Telegram demo receipts distinct, checks separate from new findings, workflow activation separate from configuration, and server credentials out of the frontend.
